Why You'll Love It

Wings of Fire isn't just another dragon story. Here's what makes it stick.

🐉

A found family that actually feels like one

Five dragonets from five different tribes, raised apart and thrown together by a prophecy none of them asked for. They argue, they get jealous, they make terrible plans — and then they risk everything for each other anyway. Clay, Tsunami, Glory, Starflight and Sunny are the kind of group that feels like your friends by page fifty.

🌍

A world with real rules

Pyrrhia isn't a backdrop — it's a system. MudWings hold their breath for an hour and breathe fire when they're warm enough. SandWings radiate warmth and live on very little food and water. RainWings get more than half their energy from the sun. SeaWings breathe and see underwater. Every tribe has its own strengths, its own politics and its own version of what counts as home. You learn the world by learning the dragons who live in it.

✨

Magic that costs something

Animus magic is inherited, not chosen, and it comes with a price. Darkstalker cast a plague on the IceWings that killed almost the whole tribe — Queen Glacier among them. The Othermind's breath of evil turned Pantala's jungle into a poisoned wasteland. The good magic in this world is rarer and more precious because of what it takes.

📖

Prophecies you can argue with

The Dragonet Prophecy is the spine of the whole series — and the dragonets spend the first arc discovering it was faked. Then the prophecy still comes true anyway, just not the way anyone planned. Every arc bends the legend without breaking it.

🗺️

Two continents, one war

You get Pyrrhia's mountains, mudflats and rainforests, then cross to Pantala's silk cities, poisoned jungles and hives of insect dragons with their own queen, their own economy and their own version of the same fight. The scope keeps growing without losing the small, personal stakes.

💛

Room to grow up

These are dragons learning to be leaders while still figuring out who they are. Books one and two are about hatching and surviving; by book sixteen, Umber is deciding whether he's a hero at all, and book seventeen puts Kinkajou in the middle of the story. The growth is the whole point.

Meet the Dragonets

The five who were chosen — whether they wanted to be or not.

🐢

Clay

MudWing

Big, loyal, kind-hearted MudWing with massive brown wings and fireproof scales. The largest of the dragonets of destiny — and the one who nearly went through with a monstrous plan to kill the queen.

🦎

Tsunami

SeaWing

A fierce, hot-headed SeaWing who returns to the Kingdom of the Sea to claim the throne that was stolen from her as an egg. She is the dragonets' boldest swimmer.

🌧️

Glory

RainWing

Elegant, vain, and secretly the best fighter in the group. Raised by the RainWings of the Rainforest Kingdom, Glory is sharper and far more dangerous than anyone expects — and she has venom.

🦇

Starflight

NightWing

Smart, curious, and a little too clever for his own good. A NightWing blinded by a volcanic eruption, Starflight maps the world, reads human books, and survives on sheer brainpower.

🌵

Sunny

SandWing–NightWing hybrid

Small, fierce, and the smallest of the dragonets of destiny. The sand-scarred fighter of the group, Sunny is a SandWing–NightWing hybrid and always the last to give up.

Start Reading

Three good ways in, depending on how much time you have.

🐢 Read one arc

Books 1–5 make a complete story: five dragonets, one false prophecy, one very real ending. If you only read these, you've read the series at its most focused — and you'll want to know what happens next.

Best bet: The Dragonet Prophecy → The Lost Heir → The Hidden Kingdom → The Dark Secret → The Brightest Night

🌗 Read one book

Each main book follows a different dragonet, so any single one gives you a complete arc. The Brightest Night is the most self-contained — it ends the SandWing succession war and the first arc's plot in one book.

Best bet: The Brightest Night, or Escaping Peril if you want a change of pace

🌟 Read the whole thing

Sixteen books published and a seventeenth in the works, five Winglets and two Legends, in publication order. The Wings of Fire books are rated for ages 7–12 by Scholastic — but the story gets darker and the stakes get higher as it goes, so older readers tend to love it just as much.

Best bet: the full reading order, starting at book one and going in order

About the Author

✍️ Tui T. Sutherland

Tui Tamara Sutherland was born on July 31, 1978, in Caracas, Venezuela, and grew up in Massachusetts with her younger sister Kari, sharing original stories and exploring the classics together. She's written more than sixty books, and her books have sold over 67 million copies worldwide. Wings of Fire alone has sold over 30 million copies — one of the most successful fantasy series for young readers anywhere.

Here's the fun part: before dragons, she wrote about cats. Tui is one of the writers behind the shared Erin Hunter pen name and wrote for both the Warriors and Seekers series — so the same mind that gave us Pyrrhia also helped give us the Warrior Clans. If you're a Warriors fan, you're in good company.

She also won $46,200 on Jeopardy! in 2009 — because of course she did.

📊 By the numbers

Wings of Fire has been on the New York Times Best Seller list for over 300 weeks. Scholastic rates the books for ages 7–12, and adults get into them too — the series has proven popular well beyond its target age. Alongside the novels there's a whole shelf of extras: graphic novel adaptations, an official coloring book, a how-to-draw book, a stained-glass art book, a sticker book, a glow-in-the-dark coloring book, and even papercraft flyers that really fly.

There's also an animated series in development at Amazon MGM Studios, with Tui Sutherland as co-writer and executive producer. The original Netflix version — with Ava DuVernay attached — was scrapped in 2022, and fans were not quiet about it.

And it's still going. The Forgotten Isles Prophecy arc opened with The Hybrid Prince in 2026, and two more books are planned.
